# HeyGen On-Camera Script (Template)

Total target: 2-3 minutes of clean footage. Read Part 1 verbatim with your own name substituted (HeyGen requires the consent statement word-for-word). Speak Part 2 naturally - have the script visible, glance at it, do not stare-and-read.

> **Customization note**: Substitute `<YOUR FULL NAME>` in Part 1. Replace the placeholder content blocks in Part 2 with content drawn from your actual work and voice. The model trains on what you film, so generic content produces generic results.

---

## Part 1 - Consent statement (read verbatim, ~20 seconds)

Look directly at the camera lens. Speak clearly and at a normal pace.

> Hi, my name is `<YOUR FULL NAME>`. I am authorizing HeyGen to use this video to create my AI avatar. I confirm that I am the person in this video, and that I am over the age of eighteen. I grant HeyGen permission to use my likeness, voice, and image to train an avatar model that I will use for content I produce.

After reading the statement, pause for two seconds before continuing.

---

## Part 2 - Natural-talk content (speak naturally, ~2 minutes)

This trains the avatar's range of expression, head movement, and gesture vocabulary. Move your hands as you naturally would when explaining something. Don't sit frozen.

Cover all four blocks. Each block is a different register, which gives the model variety to draw from. Replace each block's content with your own version of that register before recording. The bracketed prompts tell you what to aim for.

### Block A - Introduction and warm tone (about 30 seconds)

Speak as if you're meeting someone for the first time on a Zoom call. Introduce yourself, what you do, who you do it for, and what you find interesting about your work.

> *Example placeholder.* Hey, I'm `<your name>`. I do `<what you do>` for `<the audience or industry you serve>`. The thing that keeps me coming back to this work is `<one specific aspect you genuinely find interesting>`. I've been at it for `<duration>`, and the lesson I keep relearning is `<a small, true observation>`.

Pause two seconds.

### Block B - Confident and direct (about 30 seconds)

Speak as if you're delivering a clear, opinionated point to a team or audience that needs to hear it. Pick something you actually believe.

> *Example placeholder.* Here is the part most people get wrong about `<your topic>`. They assume `<common misconception>`. The reality is `<your corrected view>`. I have watched this play out enough times that I no longer hedge on it. If you only remember one thing from this conversation, remember that.

Pause two seconds.

### Block C - Curious and thinking out loud (about 30 seconds)

Speak as if you're working through an idea in real time. Slightly slower, more pauses, occasional head tilts. Think out loud.

> *Example placeholder.* Something I keep coming back to lately is `<a question or pattern you've been noticing>`. On the surface, it looks like `<the obvious framing>`. But the more I sit with it, the more I think the actual question is `<your reframing>`. That distinction sounds small, but it changes how I would approach the whole problem.

Pause two seconds.

### Block D - Closing and inviting (about 30 seconds)

Speak as if you're wrapping up a conversation and pointing the listener to a next step. Smile lightly at the right moments.

> *Example placeholder.* If anything I said sounds familiar, I would love to talk. The work I do is exactly this kind of work. We `<your typical engagement format - workshop, audit, consulting call, etc.>`, we `<the concrete outcome>`, and we move quickly. Reach out at `<your contact channel>`, and let's see if we can help. Thanks for watching.

---

## End of recording

Stop the camera. Save the file as one continuous MP4. Run through the QA checklist in heygen-avatar-brief.md before uploading.

## Notes for delivery

- Don't memorize. Read each block twice on paper first to get the rhythm, then talk it through. Slight word changes are fine and actually better, because the model trains on your real speaking pattern, not on you reciting.
- Part 1 (consent) MUST be word-for-word, with your full legal name substituted. HeyGen reviewers compare against their template.
- It's OK to start over. Better to do five takes than to upload a stiff one.
- Do NOT script out gestures. Just talk. Your hands will do what they always do.
- Keep eyes on the lens, not on the script. Glance down for a beat, then look back up. The avatar models eye-contact behavior from this footage.
