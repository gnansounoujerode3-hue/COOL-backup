# PVC Reading Script (Template)

Read this aloud in one continuous session. Total target: 35-45 minutes of clean recorded audio. Do NOT skip parts. Each section trains a different aspect of the voice model.

Read at your normal speaking pace. Pretend you're explaining to a smart friend over coffee. NOT podcast-radio voice. NOT slow-and-careful voice. Your real cadence.

If you stumble: pause two seconds, then re-read the entire sentence cleanly. The two-second gap lets you splice cleanly later if needed.

Stay hydrated. Take the breaks listed in the brief. If your voice tightens, stop and resume another day rather than push through.

> **Customization note**: Parts 1 and 3 are speaker-agnostic and can be read as-is. Parts 2 and 4 you should personalize before recording so the model trains on your real vocabulary, not generic prose. Look for the `<...>` placeholders.

## Part 2 - Your voice content (read for ~15 minutes)

**Customize this part before recording.** Write 12-15 paragraphs (about 15 min of speech) about your domain, expertise, opinions, and how you typically explain things to your audience. Speak in YOUR vocabulary, not in someone else's. The model trains on what you put here.

Topics that make a strong PVC training set:
- A position you hold strongly (about your industry, craft, customers, or work approach)
- A story you tell often (origin, lesson learned, customer moment)
- A framework or method you teach
- A common misunderstanding you correct
- A how-you-think-about-X explanation

Aim for prose that sounds like you talking, not like marketing copy. Write the way you talk. Contractions, asides, sentence fragments, all OK.

Two short generic placeholders are below so you have something to read if you don't want to write your own. Replace them with your own content for best results.

## Part 3 - Emotional range (read for ~5 minutes)

Each section gets a different register. You are training the model to do these on demand. Read each block twice if the first feels stiff.

**Energetic and excited (read like you are pitching the best idea you have had this year):**

This is the thing. This is exactly the thing. Look at the numbers. We just shipped this on Tuesday and by Friday we had thirty-seven new sign-ups. That is not a fluke. That is the loop working. We have been talking about this for months and now we have proof. I am telling you, this changes the entire roadmap. We need to double down right now, not next quarter.

**Calm and reassuring (read like you are talking a friend off a ledge):**

Take a breath. The deal is not lost. It feels lost because you sent the email two days ago and you have not heard back. That is not a signal. That is normal. People are busy. Wait until Monday. If you have not heard by Monday morning, send a soft follow-up. One sentence. Not three paragraphs. The deal is fine. You are fine.

**Direct and serious (read like you are giving feedback that needs to land):**

I want to be honest with you about what I am seeing. The work has been slipping for the last three weeks. I am not asking you to explain. I am telling you that I noticed, and that I need it to stop. Whatever is going on, we can talk about it. But the work has to come back to where it was. That is not negotiable. We will figure out the rest together.

**Curious and thinking out loud (read like you are working through a problem in real time):**

So if I follow this through, the issue is not actually the funnel. The funnel is doing its job. The issue is what happens after the funnel. We are getting people in, they are filling out the form, and then they are sitting in a queue for nine days before anyone calls them. By day nine they are gone. So the fix is not more leads. The fix is faster response. That is interesting. That is actually a different problem than I thought we had.

**Storytelling (read like you are telling someone over dinner about something that happened):**

So I show up at the meeting, twenty minutes early, because I am one of those people. The receptionist gives me a coffee. I sit down. Twenty minutes turns into forty. Now it is ten minutes after the meeting was supposed to start and nobody is around. I check my email. Empty. I check the calendar invite. Empty. Then I realize I am sitting in the wrong office. Wrong floor, wrong building, wrong company. I had walked into someone else's lobby and the receptionist was just being polite.
